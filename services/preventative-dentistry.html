Our Preventative Services

Care That Catches Problems Early

Dental Exams & Cleanings

Routine exams and professional cleanings are the foundation of a healthy mouth. During your exam, we check your teeth, gums, and bite for signs of decay, wear, and other concerns. Your cleaning removes plaque and tartar buildup that brushing and flossing at home can miss, helping protect against cavities and gum disease.

Most patients do well with a visit about every six months, and we’ll recommend a schedule based on your own needs.

Dental Sealants

Sealants are thin protective coatings applied to the chewing surfaces of the back teeth, where deep grooves can trap food and bacteria. The application is quick and requires no drilling. Sealants are especially helpful for children and teens as their permanent molars come in, though adults can benefit too.

Fluoride Treatment

Fluoride strengthens tooth enamel and helps make it more resistant to decay. A professional fluoride treatment takes only minutes during your visit and delivers more fluoride than everyday toothpaste and rinses. It’s a simple way to give children’s developing teeth, and adult teeth at higher risk of cavities, extra protection.

Gum Disease Treatment

Gum disease often develops quietly, with few symptoms in its early stages. Bleeding, swollen, or tender gums, persistent bad breath, and gums that pull away from the teeth are all signs worth checking. We screen for gum disease at your exams and offer treatment, which may include a deeper cleaning of the teeth and gum line, to help bring it under control.

Caught early, gum disease is far easier to manage, and healthy gums are essential to keeping your natural teeth for the long term.

Oral Cancer Screening

An oral cancer screening is part of a thorough dental exam. We look for unusual spots, sores, lumps, or color changes in the mouth, on the tongue, and around the throat. Finding oral cancer early makes a meaningful difference in treatment, which is why screening matters even when nothing seems wrong.

Mouth Guards / Sport Guards

If you or your child plays contact sports or activities like basketball, soccer, or skateboarding, a mouth guard helps protect teeth, lips, and gums from injury. A guard that fits your mouth is more comfortable to wear and stays in place better than a one-size-fits-all option from the sporting goods store.

Night Guards

Many people clench or grind their teeth during sleep without realizing it, often waking with jaw soreness, headaches, or sensitive teeth. Over time, grinding can wear down or chip teeth. A night guard creates a protective barrier between your upper and lower teeth while you sleep.

If you notice these symptoms, or a partner hears you grinding, let us know at your next visit.

Common Questions

Preventative Dentistry FAQs

How often should I get a dental cleaning and exam?

Most patients do well with a visit about every six months. Some people, such as those with gum disease or a higher risk of cavities, benefit from more frequent visits. We’ll recommend a schedule that fits you.

Is preventative dental care covered by insurance?

Many dental plans cover routine exams and cleanings, though coverage varies. Focus Dental Studio is in-network with most dental insurance plans, and our team can check your benefits and provide out-of-pocket estimates before any procedure.

What if I don’t have dental insurance?

Our Focus Care membership offers reduced prices on dental care with no waiting periods, no claim forms, and no annual maximums.

When should my child first see the dentist?

Dental organizations generally recommend a first visit by a child’s first birthday. Early visits help children get comfortable and let us spot concerns sooner. Learn more about our pediatric dentistry.

What should I expect at my first visit?

We’ll review your health history and any concerns, complete an exam and cleaning as appropriate, and talk through what we find and your options. We aim for a warm, unhurried visit, and questions are always welcome.

Do I need to see the dentist if my teeth feel fine?

Yes. Many dental problems, including cavities, gum disease, and oral cancer, can develop without pain or obvious symptoms in the early stages. Regular visits help us find and treat them sooner, which is usually simpler and more comfortable.
